📊 How it is calculated — professional methodology

  • Manual (top priority): if you assign hours manually to a line item (journeyman / helper / equipment per unit), that value overrides any breakdown, catalog or cost-based estimate. It is saved for this estimate only.
  • Breakdown (measured): each row with a time unit (h, day…) multiplies rate × quantity. Labor rows are classified by role (journeyman / helper / foreman) from their description; the rest with a time unit count as equipment.
  • Catalog (measured): if the line item comes from the catalog and has no breakdown with hours, its estimated time is used. If the catalog has a breakdown by trade, that is kept; otherwise it is split 60% journeyman / 40% helper.
  • From cost (estimated): if none of the above applies, hours ≈ labor cost ÷ average rate of {{ "%.2f"|format(tiempos.tarifa_hora_media) }} $/h (configurable in Settings).
  • Critical duration: per line item it is the maximum among journeyman, helper, foreman and equipment (work in parallel within the same line item). Total duration is the sum of durations — the shortest schedule with one full crew and no overlap between line items.
  • Crews and composition: each line item is recalculated according to the composition chosen. With 2 journeymen the journeyman work is split; with 2 helpers the helper work is split; with n identical crews the resulting schedule is divided by n. Use the simulator above for projections.
  • Why does adding a helper push it from 36h to 50h? Because man-hours is effort (sum: 30h journeyman + 20h helper = 50h), while schedule is duration (max: 30h). With a helper, effort goes up a little but the schedule drops from 36h to 30h. The field h/unit is consumption (hours per unit): 0.5 h/m² = 30 min per m². A higher number is slower. It is not productivity (m²/h).
💡 Professional tip: fill in the line items without data first, using manual times (even approximate ones). With coverage above 90%, the schedule estimate and the labor load (journeyman-days / helper-days) are reliable enough to plan purchases and subcontractors.
